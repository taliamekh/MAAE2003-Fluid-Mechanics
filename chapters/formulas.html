    Ch 1 — Fluid Properties & Pressure

    Density (Continuum Definition) #1
    \[ \htmlData{t=ρ — fluid density [kg/m³]}{\color{#2E7D32}{\rho}} = \lim_{\htmlData{t=δV — elemental volume [m³]}{\color{#1565C0}{\delta V}} \to \delta V^*} \frac{\htmlData{t=δm — elemental mass [kg]}{\color{#D32F2F}{\delta m}}}{\htmlData{t=δV — elemental volume [m³]}{\color{#1565C0}{\delta V}}} \]

    What it means: Density is mass per unit volume at the continuum scale — the smallest volume \(\delta V^* \sim 10^{-9}\) mm³ where molecular fluctuations average out.

    Origin: Continuum assumption — at volumes larger than \(\delta V^*\), density is a smooth function of position.

    When to use: Any problem requiring fluid density as a point property. Underlies continuity, momentum, energy equations.

    When NOT to use: Rarefied gas dynamics where mean free path ≈ characteristic length.

    Use cases: "Find density of water at 20°C" → \(\rho = 998\) kg/m³. "Is continuum valid?" → check length scale ≫ mean free path.

    \[ \rho = \lim_{\delta V \to \delta V^*} \frac{\delta m}{\delta V} \]

    Mass per unit volume at continuum scale. Valid when problem length ≫ mean free path.

    \[ \rho = \lim_{\delta V \to \delta V^*} \frac{\delta m}{\delta V} \]

    Ch 2 — Hydrostatic Forces & Buoyancy

    Resultant Force — Inclined Plane #19
    \[ \htmlData{t=F — resultant hydrostatic force [N or lbf]}{\color{#D32F2F}{F}} = \htmlData{t=p_CG — pressure at the centroid [Pa]}{\color{#D32F2F}{p_{\text{CG}}}} \cdot \htmlData{t=A — area of the submerged surface [m² or ft²]}{\color{#1565C0}{A}} = \left(\htmlData{t=pₐ — surface pressure (atmospheric) [Pa]}{\color{#D32F2F}{p_a}} + \htmlData{t=γ — specific weight of the fluid [N/m³]}{\color{#2E7D32}{\gamma}} \, \htmlData{t=h_CG — vertical depth of centroid below free surface [m]}{\color{#1565C0}{h_{\text{CG}}}}\right) \htmlData{t=A — area of the submerged surface [m² or ft²]}{\color{#1565C0}{A}} \]

    What it means: The total hydrostatic force on a flat submerged surface equals the pressure evaluated at the centroid times the surface area — as if the entire surface were at the centroid depth.

    Origin: Integration of \(p\,dA\) over the surface using \(\int h\,dA = h_{\text{CG}} A\) by definition of centroid.

    When to use: Any plane surface (vertical, inclined, or horizontal) submerged in a static fluid. For surfaces open to atmosphere, set \(p_a = 0\) (gage): \(F = \gamma h_{\text{CG}} A\).

    When NOT to use: Curved surfaces (use horizontal/vertical component method). Moving fluids (not hydrostatic). Non-planar surfaces.

    Use cases: "Find force on a dam face" → identify \(h_{\text{CG}}\), compute \(F\). "Force on a gate" → same, then use CP for equilibrium.

    \[ F = (p_a + \gamma h_{\text{CG}}) A \]

    Total hydrostatic force on a plane surface. \(h_{\text{CG}}\) = vertical centroid depth. Open surface: \(F = \gamma h_{\text{CG}} A\).

    \[ F = p_{\text{CG}} A = (p_a + \gamma h_{\text{CG}}) A \]
    Center of Pressure — \(y_{\text{CP}}\) #20
    \[ \htmlData{t=y_CP — distance from centroid to center of pressure along surface (negative = below CG) [m]}{\color{#1565C0}{y_{\text{CP}}}} = -\frac{\htmlData{t=γ — specific weight [N/m³]}{\color{#2E7D32}{\gamma}} \, \htmlData{t=sin θ — sine of the incline angle}{\color{#7B1FA2}{\sin\theta}} \;\; \htmlData{t=I_xx — second moment of area about centroidal x-axis [m⁴]}{\color{#00838F}{I_{xx}}}}{\htmlData{t=p_CG — pressure at the centroid [Pa]}{\color{#D32F2F}{p_{\text{CG}}}} \;\; \htmlData{t=A — area of the surface [m²]}{\color{#1565C0}{A}}} \]

    What it means: The center of pressure (where the resultant acts) is always displaced below the centroid toward the high-pressure side. The negative sign confirms this.

    Origin: Moment balance of distributed pressure about the centroid: \(F \cdot y_{\text{CP}} = \int y \, p \, dA\).

    When to use: After finding \(F\), locate CP for moment/equilibrium analysis. Gage form: \(y_{\text{CP}} = -I_{xx}\sin\theta / (h_{\text{CG}} A)\). Rectangle: \(I_{xx} = bL^3/12\). Circle: \(I_{xx} = \pi R^4/4\).

    When NOT to use: Horizontal surfaces (\(\theta=0°\), CP = CG). Curved surfaces (different method).

    Use cases: "Where does force act on a gate?" → find \(y_{\text{CP}}\), locate CP below CG. "Find hinge reaction" → moment about hinge using CP.

    \[ y_{\text{CP}} = -\frac{\gamma \sin\theta \, I_{xx}}{p_{\text{CG}} A} \]

    CP below centroid. Gage: \(y_{\text{CP}} = -I_{xx}\sin\theta/(h_{\text{CG}} A)\). Rect: \(I_{xx}=bL^3/12\). Circle: \(I_{xx}=\pi R^4/4\). CP always below CG.

    \[ y_{\text{CP}} = -\frac{\gamma \sin\theta \, I_{xx}}{p_{\text{CG}} A} \qquad x_{\text{CP}} = -\frac{\gamma \sin\theta \, I_{xy}}{p_{\text{CG}} A} \]
