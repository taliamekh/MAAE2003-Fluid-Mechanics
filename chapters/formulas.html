    Ch 1 — Fluid Properties & Pressure

    Density #1
    \[ \textcolor{#2E7D32}{\rho} = \frac{\textcolor{#E65100}{m}}{\textcolor{#E65100}{V}} \]

    The mass of fluid per unit volume — the most fundamental intensive property.

    ✓ Use when
    asked for mass per volume, need ρ for continuity / momentum / energy equations.
    ✗ Not for
    weight per volume — that is γ = ρg. Do not confuse with SG (dimensionless).

    Mass per unit volume. SI: kg/m³. BG: slugs/ft³.

    Specific Weight #2
    \[ \textcolor{#2E7D32}{\gamma} = \textcolor{#2E7D32}{\rho} \, \textcolor{#2E7D32}{g} \]

    Weight of fluid per unit volume — density scaled by gravity.

    ✓ Use when
    hydrostatic pressure (p = γh), buoyancy, manometers.
    ✗ Not for
    interchangeable with density — γ includes gravity. γ_water = 9810 N/m³.

    Weight per unit volume. SI: N/m³. BG: lbf/ft³.

    Specific Gravity #3
    \[ \textcolor{#2E7D32}{SG} = \frac{\textcolor{#2E7D32}{\rho}}{\textcolor{#1565C0}{\rho_W}} \]

    Dimensionless ratio of a fluid's density to water's at 4°C.

    ✓ Use when
    quick comparison with water, buoyancy (SG > 1 sinks), manometer multi-fluid problems.
    ✗ Not for
    plugging directly into equations — convert: ρ = SG × ρ_W first.

    Dimensionless density ratio. Reference: water at 4°C.

    Newton's Law of Viscosity #4
    \[ \textcolor{#D32F2F}{\tau} = \textcolor{#2E7D32}{\mu} \frac{d\textcolor{#E65100}{u}}{d\textcolor{#1565C0}{y}} \]

    Shear stress is proportional to velocity gradient. The constant μ is the dynamic viscosity.

    ✓ Use when
    finding shear force on plates, determining viscosity from experiment, pipe flow wall shear.
    ✗ Not for
    non-Newtonian fluids (variable μ). Not for turbulent flow.

    Shear stress = viscosity × velocity gradient. Newtonian fluids only.

    Kinematic Viscosity #5
    \[ \textcolor{#2E7D32}{\nu} = \frac{\textcolor{#2E7D32}{\mu}}{\textcolor{#2E7D32}{\rho}} \]

    Dynamic viscosity normalized by density. Has dimensions of diffusivity (m²/s).

    ✓ Use when
    Reynolds number: Re = VL/ν. Comparing fluids of different densities.
    ✗ Not for
    finding shear stress — need μ for that (τ = μ du/dy).

    Viscosity normalized by density. Used in Reynolds number.

    Droplet / Bubble Pressure #6
    \[ \textcolor{#D32F2F}{\Delta p} = \frac{2\textcolor{#2E7D32}{\Upsilon}}{\textcolor{#E65100}{R}} \;\text{(droplet)} \qquad \frac{4\textcolor{#2E7D32}{\Upsilon}}{\textcolor{#E65100}{R}} \;\text{(bubble)} \]

    Surface tension creates internal pressure. Bubbles have two surfaces → 2× droplet pressure.

    ✓ Use when
    pressure inside droplets, bubbles, or curved menisci.
    ✗ Not for
    non-spherical geometries. Gravity effects neglected.

    Pressure excess inside curved surfaces from surface tension.

    Capillary Height #7
    \[ \textcolor{#1565C0}{h} = \frac{2\textcolor{#2E7D32}{\Upsilon} \textcolor{#7B1FA2}{\cos\theta}}{\textcolor{#2E7D32}{\gamma} \, \textcolor{#E65100}{R}} \]

    Height a liquid rises or falls in a narrow tube. Positive for wetting (θ < 90°), negative for non-wetting.

    ✓ Use when
    capillary tube problems, correcting manometer readings in small tubes.
    ✗ Not for
    non-circular tubes. Requires known θ and Υ.

    Capillary rise. Positive = wetting, negative = non-wetting.

    Ideal Gas Law #8
    \[ \textcolor{#D32F2F}{p} = \textcolor{#2E7D32}{\rho} \, \textcolor{#E65100}{R} \, \textcolor{#E65100}{T} \]

    Equation of state for ideal gases — relates absolute pressure, density, and absolute temperature.

    ✓ Use when
    gas density at given conditions, compressible flow, atmosphere problems.
    ✗ Not for
    very high p or low T (real gas). Must use absolute p and T — never gauge or °C.

    Ideal gas EOS. Use absolute pressure (Pa) and temperature (K).

    Absolute / Gage Pressure #9
    \[ \textcolor{#D32F2F}{p_{\text{abs}}} = \textcolor{#D32F2F}{p_{\text{gage}}} + \textcolor{#D32F2F}{p_{\text{atm}}} \]

    Absolute = total from vacuum. Gage = above atmospheric. Vacuum = below atmospheric.

    ✓ Use when
    converting gage ↔ absolute, ideal gas law (needs absolute p).
    ✗ Not for
    p_gage can be negative (vacuum). p_abs is never negative.

    Absolute = gage + atmospheric. p_atm ≈ 101.325 kPa.

    Hydrostatic Pressure #10
    \[ \textcolor{#D32F2F}{p} = \textcolor{#D32F2F}{p_a} + \textcolor{#2E7D32}{\gamma} \, \textcolor{#1565C0}{h} \]

    Pressure increases linearly with depth in a static incompressible fluid.

    ✓ Use when
    pressure at depth, manometer analysis, pressure on submerged surfaces.
    ✗ Not for
    compressible fluids (γ varies). Not for moving fluids.

    Pressure at depth h. Linear in incompressible fluid.

    Manometer Equation #11
    \[ \textcolor{#D32F2F}{p_A} - \textcolor{#D32F2F}{p_B} = \sum \textcolor{#2E7D32}{\gamma_i} \, \textcolor{#1565C0}{\Delta h_i} \]

    Walk A→B through manometer: go down → add γΔh, go up → subtract γΔh.

    ✓ Use when
    pressure difference via U-tube or multi-fluid manometer.
    ✗ Not for
    dynamic flows. Down adds pressure, up subtracts.

    Walk through: +γΔh down, −γΔh up.

    Ch 2 — Hydrostatic Forces & Buoyancy

    Resultant Hydrostatic Force #12
    \[ \textcolor{#D32F2F}{F_R} = \textcolor{#2E7D32}{\gamma} \, \textcolor{#1565C0}{\bar{h}} \, \textcolor{#1565C0}{A} \]

    Total force on a submerged plane surface = pressure at centroid × area.

    ✓ Use when
    total force on a gate, dam, tank wall, or any flat submerged surface.
    ✗ Not for
    curved surfaces (project onto planes). \(\bar{h}\) is vertical depth, not along surface.

    Resultant force = pressure at centroid × area.

    Center of Pressure #13
    \[ \textcolor{#1565C0}{y_{cp}} = \textcolor{#1565C0}{\bar{y}} + \frac{\textcolor{#00838F}{I_{xx}}}{\textcolor{#1565C0}{\bar{y}} \, \textcolor{#1565C0}{A}} \]

    Center of pressure is always below the centroid. Offset I_xx/(ȳA) is always positive.

    ✓ Use when
    finding where F_R acts — needed for moment/equilibrium on gates, hinges.
    ✗ Not for
    \(\bar{y}\) is along the incline from free-surface line, not vertical. Use centroidal I_xx.

    Center of pressure = centroid + offset. Always below centroid.
