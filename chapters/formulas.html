Ch 1 — Fluid Properties & Pressure

Density (Continuum Definition)
\[ \rho = \lim_{\delta V \to \delta V^*} \frac{\delta m}{\delta V} \]

What it means: Density is the mass per unit volume, defined at the smallest volume element \(\delta V^*\) where molecular fluctuations average out and a stable macroscopic value emerges.

Origin: Derived from the continuum assumption — at volumes larger than \(\delta V^* \sim 10^{-9}\) mm³ (containing ~\(3 \times 10^7\) molecules of air at STP), density is a smooth function of position, enabling differential calculus.

When to use: Any problem requiring fluid density as a point property. Underlies every governing equation in the course — continuity, momentum, energy.

When NOT to use: Does not apply at molecular scales (e.g., rarefied gas dynamics where mean free path ≈ characteristic length). Also breaks down at macroscopic scales where spatial non-uniformity matters (use local density instead of bulk average).

Use cases: "Find the density of water at 20°C" → look up \(\rho = 998\) kg/m³. "Is the continuum assumption valid?" → check if the problem length scale ≫ mean free path.

\[ \rho = \lim_{\delta V \to \delta V^*} \frac{\delta m}{\delta V} \]

Mass per unit volume at the continuum scale. Valid when problem length scale ≫ mean free path (~\(10^{-9}\) mm³ threshold).

\[ \rho = \lim_{\delta V \to \delta V^*} \frac{\delta m}{\delta V} \]

Ch 2 — Hydrostatic Forces & Buoyancy

Resultant Force — Inclined Plane #19
\[ F = p_{\text{CG}} \, A = (p_a + \gamma h_{\text{CG}}) \, A \]

What it means: The total hydrostatic force on a flat submerged surface equals the pressure at its centroid multiplied by its area — as if the entire surface were at the centroid depth.

Origin: Integration of \(p\,dA\) over the surface, using \(\int h\,dA = h_{\text{CG}} A\) by definition of centroid.

When to use: Any plane surface (vertical, inclined, or horizontal) submerged in a static fluid. For open surfaces, set \(p_a = 0\) (gage): \(F = \gamma h_{\text{CG}} A\).

When NOT to use: Curved surfaces (use component method instead — see Ch 2.2). Moving fluids (not hydrostatic).

Use cases: "Find the force on a dam face" → identify \(h_{\text{CG}}\), compute \(F\). "Force on a gate" → same, then use CP for moment analysis.

\[ F = (p_a + \gamma h_{\text{CG}}) A \]

Total hydrostatic force on a plane surface. \(h_{\text{CG}}\) = centroid depth. For open surfaces: \(F = \gamma h_{\text{CG}} A\).

\[ F = p_{\text{CG}} A = (p_a + \gamma h_{\text{CG}}) A \]
Center of Pressure #20
\[ y_{\text{CP}} = -\frac{\gamma \sin\theta \, I_{xx}}{p_{\text{CG}} A} \qquad x_{\text{CP}} = -\frac{\gamma \sin\theta \, I_{xy}}{p_{\text{CG}} A} \]

What it means: The center of pressure is where the resultant force effectively acts. It is always displaced toward the high-pressure (deeper) side of the surface relative to the centroid.

Origin: Moment balance of the distributed pressure about the centroid. \(I_{xx}\), \(I_{xy}\) are 2nd moments of area about centroidal axes of the surface.

When to use: After finding \(F\), use CP to locate the force for moment/equilibrium analysis (gates, hinges, dams). For gage pressure: \(y_{\text{CP}} = -I_{xx}\sin\theta / (h_{\text{CG}} A)\).

When NOT to use: Horizontal surfaces (\(\theta = 0\)) where CP = CG. Curved surfaces (different method).

Use cases: "Where does the force act on a gate?" → compute \(y_{\text{CP}}\), locate CP below CG. "Find hinge reactions" → use CP in moment equation.

\[ y_{\text{CP}} = -\frac{\gamma \sin\theta \, I_{xx}}{p_{\text{CG}} A} \]

Distance of CP below centroid. For gage: \(y_{\text{CP}} = -I_{xx}\sin\theta/(h_{\text{CG}} A)\). CP is always below CG. Rectangle: \(I_{xx} = bL^3/12\). Circle: \(I_{xx} = \pi R^4/4\).

\[ y_{\text{CP}} = -\frac{\gamma \sin\theta \, I_{xx}}{p_{\text{CG}} A} \qquad x_{\text{CP}} = -\frac{\gamma \sin\theta \, I_{xy}}{p_{\text{CG}} A} \]
