🧩 Quick-solve guides

Step recipes for each shape. Sheet references point to the provided Final Sheet.

① Type 1 — Rating (find \(h_f\) / \(\Delta p\))
  1. Velocity from flow: \(V=4Q/\pi d^2\) (continuity, sheet p.12).
  2. Reynolds number: \(Re_d=Vd/\nu\) (p.13) → state the regime.
  3. Friction factor: laminar \(f=64/Re_d\) (p.13); turbulent → \(\varepsilon/d\) → Haaland or Moody (p.13–14).
  4. Head loss: Weisbach \(h_f=f(L/d)(V^2/2g)\) (p.13).
  5. Pressure drop (horizontal, constant-area only): \(\Delta p=\gamma h_f\). (off-sheet — derive from the head equation.)
② Type 2 — Analysis (find \(Q\))
  1. Assume turbulent (verify at the end).
  2. Dimensionless head loss: \(\zeta=g\,d^3 h_f/(L\nu^2)\) (p.13).
  3. Explicit Reynolds: \(Re_d=-(8\zeta)^{1/2}\log\big(\tfrac{\varepsilon/d}{3.7}+\tfrac{1.775}{\sqrt\zeta}\big)\) (p.13).
  4. Velocity & flow: \(V=\nu Re_d/d\) (p.13), \(Q=\tfrac{\pi}{4}d^2V\).
  5. Verify \(Re_d>4000\) (else laminar — use \(f=64/Re\) directly).
③ Type 3 — Design (find \(d\))
  1. Guess \(f\approx0.02\text{–}0.03\).
  2. Diameter: \(d^5=8fLQ^2/(\pi^2 g\,h_f)\) (p.13) → \(d\).
  3. Re & roughness: \(Re_d=4Q/(\pi d\nu)\) (p.13), compute \(\varepsilon/d\).
  4. New \(f\) from Moody / Haaland; repeat from step 2 until \(f\) stops changing (2–3 passes).
④ Complete system — pump between two reservoirs
  1. Pick the two free surfaces as points 1 and 2: there \(p=0\) (gauge) and \(V\approx0\).
  2. Head equation (p.13) collapses to \(z_1+h_{\text{pump}}=z_2+h_{loss}\).
  3. Pipe velocity: \(V=Q/A\); then \(Re_d\) → regime → \(f\).
  4. Major loss: \(\sum h_f=f(L/d)(V^2/2g)\); Minor loss: list every fitting → \(\sum K\) from the p.14 table (sharp exit \(K_L=1.0\)!) → \(\sum h_m=(\sum K)V^2/2g\).
  5. Solve \(h_{\text{pump}}\), then power \(\dot W_f=\rho g Q\,h_{\text{pump}}\) (p.13); ÷ \(\eta_{\text{pump}}\) for shaft power.
⑤ Conceptual — laminar vs turbulent / which \(f\)?
  1. Compute \(Re_d=Vd/\nu\) (convert \(\mu\to\nu=\mu/\rho\) if needed).
  2. Classify: \(<2300\) laminar (\(f=64/Re\)); \(>4000\) turbulent (Haaland / Moody); \(2000\text{–}4000\) transitional.
  3. Remember roughness \(\varepsilon/d\) affects \(f\) only when turbulent.

⚡ Concept traps & tips

  • No Moody chart in laminar flow. If \(Re_d<2300\), \(f=64/Re\) — full stop.
  • Smaller-pipe velocity for area changes. Sudden expansion/contraction \(K\) uses the faster (smaller-\(d\)) stream.
  • The \((f\Sigma L/d+\Sigma K)\) factoring is single-diameter only. Different sizes ⇒ separate segments.
  • \(\Delta p=\gamma h_f\) is horizontal & constant-area only — and it's NOT on the sheet; any elevation change or machine ⇒ full head equation.
  • Don't mix \(\mu\) and \(\nu\). Reynolds uses \(\nu=\mu/\rho\).
  • Type 2 / Type 3 are turbulent-only. Always verify \(Re\) at the end.
  • Keep BG units straight. \(g=32.2\ \text{ft/s}^2\), mass in slugs.
  • The sheet gives Haaland, not Colebrook. Use Haaland (p.13) or read the Moody chart (p.14) — both are provided.

🎯 If you memorise nothing else

  • Reynolds: \(Re_d=Vd/\nu\), threshold \(\approx2300\).
  • Laminar \(f\): \(f=64/Re_d\). Major loss: \(h_f=f\dfrac{L}{d}\dfrac{V^2}{2g}\).
  • Minor loss: \(h_m=K\dfrac{V^2}{2g}\); sharp exit \(K_L=1.0\), gate valve \(0.19\), globe \(10\).
  • Total: \(h_{loss}=\sum h_f+\sum h_m\) → head eq \(\dfrac{p}{\gamma}+\dfrac{V^2}{2g}+z\) with +loss / −pump / +turbine.
  • Name by the missing variable: \(h_f\)→T1, \(Q\)→T2, \(d\)→T3, pump/2 reservoirs→system.
  • Off-sheet to memorise: \(\Delta p=\gamma h_f\) (horizontal only), and that \(\varepsilon/d\) matters only in turbulent flow.

📌 The toolkit you bring in

Pipe flow is not new theory — it is the head equation with the losses written out. These four must be automatic.

Energy / head equation (sheet p.13)
\[\frac{p_1}{\gamma}+\alpha_1\frac{V_1^2}{2g}+z_1=\frac{p_2}{\gamma}+\alpha_2\frac{V_2^2}{2g}+z_2+h_{loss}-h_{p}+h_{t}\]

Every pipe problem is this equation. Pipe flow just replaces \(h_{loss}\) with \(\sum h_f+\sum h_m\). Sign pattern: +loss, −pump, +turbine. The KE factor \(\alpha\) (≈2 laminar, ≈1.05 turbulent) is on the sheet too.

Bernoulli (sheet p.12)
\[\frac{p_1}{\gamma}+\frac{V_1^2}{2g}+z_1=\frac{p_2}{\gamma}+\frac{V_2^2}{2g}+z_2\]

The head equation with no losses and no machine — steady, incompressible, frictionless, along a streamline. As soon as a real pipe has length, Bernoulli is replaced by the head equation.

Continuity (sheet p.12)
\[Q=A\,V=\text{const}\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad V=\frac{4Q}{\pi d^2}\]

Gives the velocity every pipe formula needs. For a round pipe \(A=\tfrac{\pi}{4}d^2\), so \(V=4Q/\pi d^2\) is the workhorse substitution.

Kinematic viscosity
\[\nu=\frac{\mu}{\rho}\]

Reynolds uses \(\nu\), not \(\mu\). The sheet defines \(Re_d=Vd/\nu\) directly; if a problem gives \(\mu\) and \(\rho\), convert first (this conversion is not spelled out on the sheet).

📚 Core concepts (Ch 6)

A · Flow regimes & the Reynolds number

Two regimes

Laminar flow is ordered — smooth layers, no mixing (Reynolds' dye filament stays a thin line). Turbulent flow is chaotic — eddies mix the fluid (the dye bursts). The regime decides which friction-factor rule applies, so it is always established first.

Reynolds number (sheet p.13)
\[Re_d=\frac{\rho V d}{\mu}=\frac{V d}{\nu}\]

Ratio of inertia to viscous forces. Big \(Re\) ⇒ inertia wins ⇒ turbulent; small \(Re\) ⇒ viscosity wins ⇒ laminar.

\(\rho\) — density\(V\) — average velocity\(d\) — diameter\(\mu\) — dynamic viscosity\(\nu=\mu/\rho\) — kinematic viscosity
★ Regime thresholds

\(Re_{crit}\approx2300\). Bands: \(<2000\) laminar · \(2000\text{–}4000\) transitional · \(>4000\) turbulent. The Moody chart (p.14) marks the laminar line, critical zone, transition, and the complete-turbulence rough-pipe region. Roughness \(\varepsilon/d\) matters only when turbulent.

B · Major losses — Weisbach & the friction factor

Friction head loss \(h_f\)

The energy a fluid loses to wall friction down a straight pipe — "major" because it is distributed over the whole length. This is exactly the \(h_{loss}\) of the head equation for a plain pipe.

Weisbach / Darcy–Weisbach (sheet p.13)
\[h_f=f\,\frac{L}{d}\,\frac{V^2}{2g}\]

The master major-loss equation — works in both regimes; only the way you get \(f\) changes. This is the Darcy factor (\(4\times\) Fanning; laminar line \(64/Re\), not \(16/Re\)).

Friction factor — laminar then turbulent (sheet p.13)
\[f_{\text{lam}}=\frac{64}{Re_d}\qquad\qquad \frac{1}{\sqrt f}\approx-1.8\log\!\Big[\frac{6.9}{Re_d}+\Big(\frac{\varepsilon/d}{3.7}\Big)^{1.11}\Big]\]

Laminar: exact, roughness-independent, no chart. Turbulent: depends on \(Re_d\) and \(\varepsilon/d\) — the sheet gives the explicit Haaland form (no Colebrook), and the Moody chart (p.14) as the graphical equivalent.

★ Head loss ↔ pressure drop

\(\Delta p=\gamma h_f\) holds only for a horizontal, constant-area pipe. This shortcut is not on the sheet — if the pipe rises or changes size, go back to the head equation.

C · The three single-pipe problems

Any two of \(\{d,\ Q,\ h_f\}\) given → find the third. Name the problem by which is missing — that tells you whether the solve is direct or iterative. The sheet (p.13) lists all three.

The taxonomy

Type 1 (rating) — missing \(h_f\): everything for the march is known. Direct.
Type 2 (analysis) — missing \(Q\): \(V\) unknown ⇒ \(Re\), \(f\) unknown. Explicit ζ method.
Type 3 (design) — missing \(d\): \(d\) hides in \(Re\), \(\varepsilon/d\) and the loss equation at once. Iterative.

Type 2 — explicit (sheet p.13)
\[\zeta=\frac{g\,d^3 h_f}{L\,\nu^2}\qquad Re_d=-(8\zeta)^{1/2}\log\!\Big(\frac{\varepsilon/d}{3.7}+\frac{1.775}{\sqrt{\zeta}}\Big)\qquad V=\frac{\nu Re_d}{d}\]

\(\zeta\) is built from known quantities (no \(V\) inside), breaking the circularity. Then \(Q=\tfrac{\pi}{4}d^2V\). Turbulent only — verify \(Re_d>4000\).

Type 3 — iterative (sheet p.13)
\[d^5=\frac{8\,f\,L\,Q^2}{\pi^2\,g\,h_f}\qquad Re_d=\frac{4Q}{\pi d\,\nu}\qquad f=F(Re_d,\varepsilon/d)\]

Guess \(f\approx0.02\text{–}0.03\) → solve \(d^5\) → get \(Re_d\), \(\varepsilon/d\) → new \(f\) from Moody → repeat. Converges fast (\(d\propto f^{1/5}\)).

D · Minor losses & the complete system

Minor losses \(h_m\)

Local losses at fittings, valves, bends, entrances, exits, area changes. "Minor" = local, not small — a nearly-closed valve can lose more than a long pipe. Same \(h_{loss}\) bucket as friction.

Loss coefficient & total loss (sheet p.13)
\[h_m=K\,\frac{V^2}{2g}\qquad\qquad h_{total}=\sum f\,\frac{L}{d}\frac{V^2}{2g}+\sum K\,\frac{V^2}{2g}\]

\(K\) is dimensionless and experimental — values in the p.14 table. Feed \(h_{total}\) into the head equation. Single-diameter ⇒ factor out \(V^2/2g\); multiple sizes ⇒ per segment.

★ K values on the sheet (p.14)

Gate valve (open) \(0.19\) · globe \(10\) · angle \(5\) · ball \(0.05\) · swing check \(2\) · 90° elbow \(0.9\) · 90° long sweep \(0.6\) · 45° bend \(0.4\) · 180° return \(2\) · tee (run) \(0.4\) · tee (branch) \(1.8\). Re-entrant entrance / sharp exit \(K_L=1.0\) (dumps the full velocity head). Area changes use the smaller-pipe velocity.
