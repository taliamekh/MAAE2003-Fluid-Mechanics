This page reproduces the provided Midterm 2 equation sheet. Equations keep their position on the physical page (#1 is top-left, numbers run across each row then down). The sheet spans Ch 1–5; the examinable core for Midterm 2 is Ch 3–5 — Ch 1–2 are background you are expected to already know. For the full derivation-by-derivation reference, see the Full Reference Sheet. Click any coloured variable for its meaning.

    Row 1
    #1Ch 1Pressure
    \[ \textcolor{#D32F2F}{p} = \frac{\textcolor{#D32F2F}{F}}{\textcolor{#1565C0}{A}} \]

    Used for: converting between a normal force and the pressure it produces on a surface. Foundation for all hydrostatics.

    #2Ch 1Shear Stress
    \[ \textcolor{#D32F2F}{\tau} = \frac{\textcolor{#D32F2F}{F}}{\textcolor{#1565C0}{A}} \]

    Used for: the tangential (frictional) force a fluid exerts parallel to a surface. Pairs with Newton's law of viscosity (#7).

    Row 2
    #3Ch 1Specific Gravity (vs water)
    \[ \textcolor{#2E7D32}{SG} = \frac{\textcolor{#2E7D32}{\rho}}{\textcolor{#1565C0}{\rho_w}} \]

    Used for: density of a liquid relative to water. Recover density with ρ = SG × 1000. SG > 1 sinks in water.

    #4Ch 1Specific Gravity (vs air)
    \[ \textcolor{#2E7D32}{SG} = \frac{\textcolor{#2E7D32}{\rho}}{\textcolor{#1565C0}{\rho_a}} \]

    Used for: density of a gas relative to air. Recover gas density with ρ = SG × ρ_a.

    Row 3
    #5Ch 1Kelvin Conversion
    \[ \textcolor{#E65100}{K} = \textcolor{#E65100}{\text{°C}} + 273.16 \]

    Used for: converting Celsius to absolute temperature. Required before the ideal gas law or any ratio of temperatures.

    #6Ch 1Rankine Conversion
    \[ \textcolor{#E65100}{\text{°R}} = \textcolor{#E65100}{\text{°F}} + 459.69 \]

    Used for: absolute temperature in Imperial (BG) units — same role as Kelvin for gas relations.

    Row 4
    #7Ch 1Newton's Law of Viscosity
    \[ \textcolor{#D32F2F}{\tau} = \textcolor{#2E7D32}{\mu}\,\frac{d\textcolor{#7B1FA2}{\theta}}{dt} = \textcolor{#2E7D32}{\mu}\,\frac{d\textcolor{#1565C0}{u}}{d\textcolor{#1565C0}{y}} \]

    Used for: relating shear stress to the velocity gradient (rate of angular deformation). Gives wall shear, viscous drag, and the velocity profile in a Newtonian fluid.

    #8Ch 1Ideal Gas Law
    \[ \textcolor{#D32F2F}{p} = \textcolor{#2E7D32}{\rho}\,\textcolor{#2E7D32}{R}\,\textcolor{#E65100}{T} \]

    Used for: finding gas density from pressure and temperature (compressible flow, buoyancy of gases). T must be absolute.

    Row 5
    #9Ch 1Kinematic Viscosity
    \[ \textcolor{#2E7D32}{\nu} = \frac{\textcolor{#2E7D32}{\mu}}{\textcolor{#2E7D32}{\rho}} \]

    Used for: momentum diffusivity. Appears in the Reynolds number. The sheet's script "v" is ν (nu), not velocity.

    #10Ch 1Specific Weight
    \[ \textcolor{#2E7D32}{\gamma} = \textcolor{#2E7D32}{\rho}\,\textcolor{#2E7D32}{g} \]

    Used for: weight per unit volume. Feeds directly into hydrostatic pressure, manometers, and hydrostatic force. γ_water = 9810 N/m³.

    #11Ch 1Hydrostatic Pressure
    \[ \textcolor{#D32F2F}{P} = \textcolor{#2E7D32}{\gamma}\,\textcolor{#1565C0}{h} \]

    Used for: gauge pressure increasing linearly with depth in a static fluid. Use to find pressure at a depth or set up manometer/force problems.

    Row 6
    #12Ch 1Manometer Equation
    \[ \textcolor{#D32F2F}{p_2} = \textcolor{#D32F2F}{p_1} + \sum_{\text{down}} \textcolor{#2E7D32}{\gamma_i}\,\textcolor{#1565C0}{h_i} - \sum_{\text{up}} \textcolor{#2E7D32}{\gamma_i}\,\textcolor{#1565C0}{h_i} \]

    Used for: stepping through connected fluid columns — add γh when moving down, subtract γh when moving up. Relates pressures across single- or multi-fluid manometers.

    Row 7
    #13Ch 2Resultant Hydrostatic Force (plane surface)
    \[ \textcolor{#D32F2F}{F} = \left(\textcolor{#D32F2F}{p_a} + \textcolor{#2E7D32}{\gamma}\,\textcolor{#1565C0}{h_{CG}}\right)\textcolor{#1565C0}{A} = \textcolor{#D32F2F}{p_{CG}}\,\textcolor{#1565C0}{A} \]

    Used for: total pressure force on a submerged plane surface = pressure at the centroid × area. Sizes gates, walls, and submerged plates. The line of action sits below the centroid and is found separately (pressure prism / second moment).

    Row 8
    #14Ch 4Mass Flow Rate
    \[ \textcolor{#D32F2F}{\dot{m}} = \int_A \textcolor{#2E7D32}{\rho}\,\textcolor{#1565C0}{v_n}\,d\textcolor{#1565C0}{A} \]

    Used for: mass crossing a section per second. Reduces to ṁ = ρAV for uniform flow; the core term in continuity.

    #15Ch 4Volume Flow Rate
    \[ \textcolor{#1565C0}{Q} = \textcolor{#1565C0}{\vec{v}}\,\textcolor{#1565C0}{A} \]

    Used for: discharge through a section. Q = const for incompressible flow (continuity); rearranges to give average velocity.

    Row 9
    #16Ch 3Bernoulli Equation (two-point form)
    \[ \textcolor{#D32F2F}{p_1} + \tfrac{1}{2}\textcolor{#2E7D32}{\rho}\,\textcolor{#1565C0}{\vec{v}_1}^{\,2} + \textcolor{#2E7D32}{\rho}\,\textcolor{#2E7D32}{g}\,\textcolor{#1565C0}{z_1} = \textcolor{#D32F2F}{p_2} + \tfrac{1}{2}\textcolor{#2E7D32}{\rho}\,\textcolor{#1565C0}{\vec{v}_2}^{\,2} + \textcolor{#2E7D32}{\rho}\,\textcolor{#2E7D32}{g}\,\textcolor{#1565C0}{z_2} \]

    Used for: conservation of mechanical energy along a streamline for steady, incompressible, inviscid flow. Relates pressure, velocity, and elevation between two points. Verify the assumptions before applying.

    Row 10
    #17Ch 5Reynolds Transport Theorem (general)
    \[ \frac{d\textcolor{#00838F}{B_{syst}}}{dt} = \frac{d}{dt}\int_{CV} \textcolor{#00838F}{\beta}\,\textcolor{#2E7D32}{\rho}\,dV + \int_{CS} \textcolor{#00838F}{\beta}\,\textcolor{#2E7D32}{\rho}\,\textcolor{#1565C0}{v_n}\,d\textcolor{#1565C0}{A} \]

    Used for: the master bridge from a system (Lagrangian) to a control volume (Eulerian): rate of change = storage inside the CV + net flux through the control surface (CS). dV is a volume element of the CV. Specialise β to derive mass, momentum, and energy balances (#18–#20).

    Row 11
    #18Ch 5Conservation of Mass (Continuity)
    \[ \frac{d\textcolor{#D32F2F}{m}}{dt} = 0 = \frac{d}{dt}\int_{CV} \textcolor{#2E7D32}{\rho}\,dV + \int_{CS} \textcolor{#2E7D32}{\rho}\,\textcolor{#1565C0}{v_n}\,d\textcolor{#1565C0}{A} \]

    Used for: RTT with B = mass (β = 1). Rate of mass storage in the CV plus net mass flux through the CS is zero. For steady flow the storage term drops and ∫ρvₙ dA = 0 (mass in = mass out). Use for filling/emptying tanks and pipe networks.

    Row 12
    #19Ch 5Linear Momentum
    \[ \frac{d(\textcolor{#D32F2F}{m}\,\textcolor{#1565C0}{\vec{v}})}{dt} = \sum \textcolor{#D32F2F}{F} = \frac{d}{dt}\int_{CV} \textcolor{#1565C0}{\vec{v}}\,\textcolor{#2E7D32}{\rho}\,dV + \int_{CS} \textcolor{#1565C0}{\vec{v}}\,\textcolor{#2E7D32}{\rho}\,\textcolor{#1565C0}{v_n}\,d\textcolor{#1565C0}{A} \]

    Used for: RTT with B = momentum (β = v⃗). Sum of external forces = momentum storage in the CV + net momentum flux out. Use for forces on bends, nozzles, vanes, and jets. Watch directions — it is a vector equation.

    Row 13
    #20Ch 5Angular Momentum
    \[ \frac{d(\textcolor{#1565C0}{r}\times \textcolor{#D32F2F}{m}\,\textcolor{#1565C0}{\vec{v}})}{dt} = \sum \textcolor{#00838F}{M} = \frac{d}{dt}\int_{CV} (\textcolor{#1565C0}{r}\times\textcolor{#1565C0}{\vec{v}})\,\textcolor{#2E7D32}{\rho}\,dV + \int_{CS} (\textcolor{#1565C0}{r}\times\textcolor{#1565C0}{\vec{v}})\,\textcolor{#2E7D32}{\rho}\,\textcolor{#1565C0}{v_n}\,d\textcolor{#1565C0}{A} \]

    Used for: RTT with B = angular momentum (β = r×v⃗). Sum of external moments = rate of change of angular momentum in the CV + net flux. Use for torques on rotating machinery — pumps, turbines, and sprinklers.
